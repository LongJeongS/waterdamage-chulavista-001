About the Area

About Imperial Beach

Imperial Beach was incorporated in 1956 and counted 26,137 residents at the 2020 census. It sits south of the Silver Strand, with San Diego Bay to the north, the Pacific to the west and the Tijuana River National Estuarine Research Reserve, 2,293 acres of protected wetland, wrapping around the south. The Bayshore Bikeway starts here on its loop around the bay.

Housing is low and dense: single-story cottages and duplexes from the 1940s to 1960s on slab or shallow raised foundations, 1970s and 1980s apartment buildings along Palm Avenue and Seacoast Drive, and some newer townhomes. Many lots sit a few feet above sea level, and the pier reaches about 1,491 feet into the surf.

Water comes from more than one side. Winter storms push surf and rain into the streets at the south end of Seacoast Drive, and the estuary backs up when the Tijuana River runs high. Inside, salt air eats water heaters and galvanized fittings, and the high water table keeps a slab damp after the leak above it is fixed.

Because the ground gives moisture back slowly, drying in Imperial Beach is measured rather than assumed. Readings are taken at the slab and the bottom plate, equipment runs until the numbers hold, and extraction, drying and rebuild each carry a written price before they start.
