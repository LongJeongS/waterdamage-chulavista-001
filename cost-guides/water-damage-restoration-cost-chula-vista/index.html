A slab leak under the kitchen and a washing machine hose that let go overnight can produce two very different invoices in the same Chula Vista zip code. The gap is rarely the water itself. It is how long the water sat, what it soaked into, and whether the job stops at drying or carries on through new drywall and flooring. This guide lays out the ranges the San Diego market tends to run, the factors that move a job up or down inside them, and what a written quote should show, so the first number you hear can be checked against something real. What Drives the Price

Cost Factors

How Long the Water Sat

Time is the biggest lever on the invoice. Clean water caught within hours usually means extraction, a few days of air movers and a dehumidifier, and little demolition. The same leak found after a weekend away has climbed the drywall, soaked the baseboard and possibly reached the subfloor, so porous material comes out before drying can begin. Materials left wet for more than about 48 hours can start to support mold growth, a commonly cited guideline, and that adds containment and cleaning to the scope.

Which Category of Water It Was

Industry convention labels water by its source. Category 1 is clean supply water from a pipe or a fill line. Category 2, often called gray water, carries some contamination, such as a dishwasher or washing machine discharge. Category 3, black water, is sewage and outdoor flood water. Category 3 losses sit at the top of the range because porous materials that touched the water are removed rather than dried, and the crew works under containment.

How Many Square Feet Got Wet

Mitigation is largely priced by affected area, and the San Diego market typically runs about $3 to $7.50 per square foot. A laundry room is a small footprint. A slab leak that migrates under the hallway, two bedrooms and a closet is a large one, and it needs more extraction time, more equipment and more days on site. The crew measures the wet area with a moisture meter, because water travels under flooring well past the visible edge.

What the Water Soaked Into

Tile on a slab shrugs off most clean water. Laminate swells and is almost always a loss. Carpet and pad can often be dried after a clean loss but come out after a sewage loss. Hardwood may be saved with mat drying if the crew gets there early. Lower cabinets soak water up through the toe kick and hide it.

Whether Walls, Cabinets or Subfloor Have to Be Opened

When moisture readings show water inside a wall cavity or under a floor, the crew removes baseboard, cuts drywall at a set height, pulls insulation and sometimes lifts flooring to reach it. Controlled demolition is labor, disposal and later a rebuild line item. A loss that can be dried in place without opening anything costs noticeably less than one of the same size that cannot.

Drying Equipment and Days on Site

Most residential losses take about 3 to 5 days of drying. Some take longer when water sat for days or reached subfloor, cabinets or insulation. Air movers and dehumidifiers are generally billed per unit per day, and daily moisture readings decide when they come off. A job that dries in three days costs less than the same job that needs six, which is one more reason early calls run lower.

Drying Only, or Drying Plus Rebuild

Mitigation ends with a dry, open structure: cut drywall, missing baseboard, bare slab. Rebuild is a second scope with its own price, covering drywall, paint, flooring, baseboard and cabinets. Many of the lower figures in national guides are mitigation only. When comparing numbers, ask whether the price stops at dry or includes the repairs.

What Water Damage Restoration Costs in Chula Vista Right Now

Local cost guides and the restoration companies working San Diego County typically put a minor clean-water loss caught early at roughly $1,000 to $3,000. A moderate loss with drying and some demolition tends to run $3,000 to $7,500. That is the more common South Bay call: a slab leak or a failed water heater found the next morning, drywall cut along two or three walls, insulation out, flooring lifted in one room, drying over most of a week. A major or multi-room loss, a second-floor bathroom that drained through the ceiling into the living room, typically runs $7,500 to $20,000 and up.

The 2026 San Diego figures typically sit in the upper half of each band more often than the lower, which matches California labor rates. Treat the bands as a way to sanity check a number, not as a quote.

What keeps a job honest is a written scope. After the moisture inspection, the extraction and structural drying scope is priced and written down before equipment is set. Demolition or rebuild, if it follows, is quoted in writing the same way. The number can change when the meter finds something new, and when it does, you see the change on paper before the work happens. Ask for that written quote for your own house.

The $1,000 to $3,000, $3,000 to $7,500 and $7,500 to $20,000 and up tiers are county figures and they typically hold in the South Bay.

What matters more than the zip code is time. The hours before extraction starts feed straight into the final invoice through the time factor above, so call when the water is found instead of waiting for morning. We're based in Chula Vista and work in Bonita, Otay Ranch, Imperial Beach and National City.

The other local variable is the house. A slab-on-grade home behaves differently in a water loss than a raised-foundation home. Water that would drain into a crawl space instead spreads sideways under flooring, which is why a slab leak that looks like a damp patch by the water heater often turns out to involve the hallway and the adjoining bedroom. So the whole floor gets metered, not only the wet-looking part.

Clean, Gray and Black Water Cost Differently

Restoration estimates lean on the water category because it decides what can be dried and what has to be removed. Category 1 clean water, a supply line or an ice maker line, can usually be extracted and dried in place if the crew arrives before the material breaks down. Category 2 gray water, from a dishwasher or washing machine drain, carries contamination that limits what gets saved. Carpet pad typically comes out, and porous materials that soaked are judged piece by piece.

Category 3 black water is sewage, toilet overflow past the trap, and outdoor flood water that has crossed soil, streets and storm drains. Industry practice is to remove porous materials that touched Category 3 water: carpet and pad, drywall and insulation to above the water line, and particleboard cabinets. Those materials are bagged and disposed of, and the remaining hard surfaces are cleaned and treated before drying starts. That removal and the containment around it are why a sewage backup in a small bathroom can cost as much as a larger clean-water loss.

Mold rides on the same clock. The EPA's mold cleanup guidance is to dry wet materials quickly and to treat anything that stays wet for more than about 48 hours as a candidate for mold growth, a commonly cited window that most restoration crews work against. Clean water that is extracted and put under equipment within that window rarely develops a mold problem. Water that sat under laminate for a week often does.

What Insurance Usually Looks At

Whether a policy pays for a water loss is the policy's and the adjuster's call, and no restoration company can decide that for you. What the adjuster typically wants to know is how the water got there and how fast it was dealt with. Sudden and accidental events, a burst pipe or a failed supply line, are read very differently from a slow leak that was dripping behind a wall for months. The California Department of Insurance residential insurance guide is worth reading before the adjuster calls, because it explains in plain language how homeowners policies are structured and what questions to ask about your own.

Documentation carries the claim. The crew photographs the loss before anything moves, logs moisture readings room by room on the first visit and every day after, records the equipment set, and writes down what was removed and why. That record is assembled in the format an adjuster asks for and handed to you. It does not change what the policy says, but it means the adjuster is working from measurements rather than memory.

One practical point: keep every written quote and scope, because the adjuster compares the invoice to them.

Drying Versus Rebuild: Two Scopes, Two Prices

Most of the confusion about water damage cost comes from mixing two different jobs. Mitigation is everything that stops the damage: extraction, controlled demolition, antimicrobial treatment where it is called for, and structural drying with air movers and dehumidifiers until the readings return to dry standard. Mitigation is what the tiers above mostly describe, and it is what the typical $3 to $7.50 per square foot figure measures.

Rebuild is everything that puts the house back: hanging and finishing drywall, baseboard, paint, flooring, cabinet replacement, sometimes a plumber or an electrician if a line or an outlet was in the wet wall. It is priced by trade and by material, not by the square foot of water, so a rebuild on a kitchen with custom cabinets looks nothing like a rebuild on a bedroom with carpet. It is also where permits come in; the city's building department can tell you whether the replacement work on your job needs one.

The gap between the two scopes is where a job can stall: the drying finishes, the equipment leaves, and the repairs still need a contractor. We also do the repair and rebuild work, and the list of what was removed during the drying is where that estimate starts. Whoever does it, get the rebuild price as a written quote before the rebuild starts.

Cost Guide FAQ

How much does water damage restoration cost in Chula Vista?

Most Chula Vista water losses typically land between about $1,000 and $7,500 in 2026, with major or multi-room losses running $7,500 to $20,000 and up. The lower end is a clean-water loss caught early with little demolition. The upper end involves several rooms, Category 3 water or extended drying. Rebuild of drywall and flooring is priced separately after drying. Ask for a written quote once the loss has been inspected.

How much does the drying cost without the repairs?

Mitigation, meaning extraction, any demolition and structural drying, typically runs about $3 to $7.50 per square foot of affected area in the San Diego market. For a single room caught early that usually lands in the $1,000 to $3,000 tier. It does not include putting the room back together, and a written quote will show where it stops.

How much does cleanup cost after a flood?

Flood water is Category 3, so flood restoration typically sits toward the upper part of the ranges, often the $3,000 to $7,500 tier for one level and $7,500 to $20,000 and up when several rooms took water. The extra cost comes from removing porous materials that touched the water rather than drying them, plus containment and cleaning before drying starts. Get a written quote once the water category is confirmed.

Can I dry out water damage myself?

For a small clean-water spill on tile caught right away, yes: mop, towels, a fan and a window. Once water has reached drywall, carpet pad, laminate, cabinets or the subfloor, household fans will not pull the moisture out before the commonly cited 48-hour mold window, and you have no way to confirm the wall is dry without a moisture meter.

How do restoration companies estimate water damage?

The estimator measures the wet area with a moisture meter, assigns the water category, lists what has to be removed and what can be dried, and prices the equipment by the day. Most estimates are built in line-item software that adjusters also work from. A good estimate is written down, with the drying and any repairs shown as separate sections.

Do larger restoration companies charge more for water removal?

Pricing in San Diego County typically falls inside the same $1,000 to $7,500 band for most losses whoever does the work, because companies large and small use similar line-item pricing. The difference you can check is scope: whether the price stops at dry or includes rebuild. Ask each company for a written quote and compare them line by line.

How long do water damage repairs take?

Drying usually takes about 3 to 5 days for a residential loss, longer when water sat for days or reached subfloor, cabinets or insulation. Rebuild is scheduled once moisture readings confirm the structure is dry, and its length depends on how much drywall, flooring and cabinetry has to go back. Ask for both timelines in writing when the scopes are priced.

Is it expensive to remediate mold, and can mold come back?

Mold remediation is separate work from water damage drying, and this page does not put a figure on it. The EPA notes that a moldy area smaller than about 10 square feet can often be cleaned by the homeowner. Mold can return if the moisture source is not fixed and the materials are not dried fully, which is why drying is confirmed with meter readings rather than by eye.
